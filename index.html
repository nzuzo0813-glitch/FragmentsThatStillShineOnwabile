Chapter 1

When Heaven Felt Far

I didn't wake up one day strong. I woke up aware. Aware of the patterns that kept repeating like a story no one had the courage to rewrite. I saw it in the way pain moved through generations quietly, almost invisibly, until it reached me. The same struggles. The same silence. The same wounds dressed in different names. And something in me refused to accept it. I decided, maybe too boldly, that it would end with me. Not because I felt ready. Not because I had everything figured out, but because I couldn't bear the thought of becoming another link in a chain that had already taken too much. I wanted something different, peace where there had been chaos, healing where there had been damage, and freedom where there had only been survival.

So, I chose to fight what I didn't fully understand. I called it purpose. I called it growth. I even called it faith but I didn't realise that breaking generational curses isn't a decision you make once it's a battle you live through. And battles don't come quietly. At first, it felt like progress. Small changes. Small victories. Moments when I thought, Maybe I'm really doing this. Maybe I'm becoming the person my past never allowed me to be. Then everything fell apart. Not slowly. Not gently. All at once, and there was no room left to breathe. The strength I thought I had started slipping through my hands. The clarity I once carried turned into confusion. The hope that fuelled me became harder to find, like trying to remember a dream that fades the moment you wake up.

And suddenly, I wasn't standing in purpose anymore. I was drowning in pressure. This wasn't what I expected. No one tells you that trying to break cycles can feel like they're tightening around you instead. That the moment you decide to change everything, everything might come for you at once. That's where I found myself. At my lowest. Not the kind of low you post about or talk your way out of. The kind where getting out of bed feels like a betrayal of the weight on your chest. The kind of low that doesn't look dramatic from the outside but feels unbearable on the inside. The kind where you question everything not just your strength, but your calling, your faith, and even your worth. Hope felt like a language I no longer understood. If I was trying to do something good, something necessary, why did it feel like I was being crushed by it? If I was stepping into purpose, why did it feel like I was losing myself instead of finding who I was meant to be?

I needed Him more than ever. Where was God? That was the question asked by someone whose heart was empty and hopeless. I cried my lungs out before I could force myself to fall asleep. I used to think God's absence was a location problem, as though He had stepped out of my room like nobody's business and forgotten to come back. I checked the ceiling, my surroundings, and deep within my soul. Guess what? Nothing. And it felt like He was nowhere. So, I decided that maybe He had disowned me. Maybe generational curses were louder than prayer. Maybe some of us were just born to be the "before" picture of someone else's testimony.

The prayers felt empty. The silence felt loud. And the distance felt personal. One day, it became impossible to ignore. What I didn't understand then, I am only beginning to see now, breaking generational curses is not a straight path to freedom. It is a confrontation. A tearing down before a rebuilding. A process that challenges everything you thought strength looked like because sometimes, breaking the cycle doesn't look like rising. Sometimes, it looks like falling and choosing not to stay there but I was still there. Still breathing. Still trying. Still refusing, even in my weakest moments, to go back to what I came from.

The question still stands, where was God? And maybe that's where God was. He was in the delay, not absent. Maybe God wasn't where I wanted Him to be, but where I needed Him in the part of me that didn't give up when everything else did. Not in the ease I expected, but in the endurance I didn't recognise. Not in removing the weight, but in giving me just enough strength to carry it for one more day.

Chapter 2

Chosen By Pain

I am a firstborn granddaughter. A middle child. The one who, without even realising it, carried the quiet expectation of becoming something more the one who could change everything. I grew up in a big home filled with warmth. Laughter lived in every corner. Happiness wasn't something we searched for it was simply there in the way we existed together. Being surrounded mostly by boys, I almost forgot I was a girl. There were no strict roles, no lines drawn between what I could or couldn't be. We were equal. Free. Just children growing up side by side. And for a long time, that felt like everything I could ever ask for.

Until, slowly, we started losing each other. Not by choice, but because death chose us. At first, it didn't feel real. It felt like something that happened to other families, something distant but then it kept happening. Again, and again. And suddenly, it wasn't distant anymore. It was us. One loss turned into many. An aunt, a cousin, a brother, a grandfather, and uncles. It didn't happen in a way we could prepare for. It came like a wave that wouldn't stop. Just when we thought we could breathe again, another one hit. And somewhere in the middle of all that grief, a question started growing inside me.

Are we cursed? Has God forsaken us?

That was it because how do you make sense of that kind of loss? How do you understand why the people you love are taken away, especially when it feels like you still needed them? When it feels like there were things you were supposed to do together, purposes that were never fulfilled? Grief didn't just take people. It took certainty. It took safety. We started living with questions no one knew how to answer.

Who's next?

That question followed us quietly, sitting in the background of every moment. Even in the laughter that remained, there was always something missing, something unspoken. And the fear changed shape. It wasn't just about losing someone anymore. It became personal. Who will I leave my children with one day? How do I live in a world without my parents because, at the end of everything, through all the noise and all the loss, we realised something simple and terrifying all we ever really had was each other. The only family I ever truly knew was my mother's side. And losing someone in that family was never something that simply went away. It stayed. It settled. It became part of how I lived but nothing prepared me for losing him. He wasn't just my grandfather. He was the one who held us together. Wherever he was, there was peace. There was joy. He was the kind of person who made everything feel okay, even when it wasn't.

To me, he was more than a grandfather. He was a father figure, our provider, our "bank," as we used to joke. I don't remember going to school without pocket money from him or leaving the house without seeing his face,the one that carried us through the whole day. He was constant. Reliable. Ours. And then, at twelve years old, I saw him lying lifeless in a casket.

That image never left me. It didn't feel real. It felt like a nightmare I couldn't wake up from but the reality of it hit hardest at the funeral. Standing there, surrounded by people, I realised something I wasn't ready to understand. He was gone. Not for a moment. Not for a while, but forever. We were never going to see him again. And something inside me broke in a way I couldn't explain. It felt like a part of me was buried with him. After the funeral, when everyone went home and the noise faded, that was when the silence became unbearable. The kind of silence that forces you to notice everything that is missing. The house wasn't the same. That voice we were used to hearing was gone. That face we saw every morning before school was gone. That feeling of completeness was gone, too. We were still there, but we weren't whole anymore.

And that's the thing about grief. You think that, with time, you learn to live with it. And maybe you do. You wake up, you continue, you try to adjust to a life that no longer looks the same but grief doesn't end. It waits. And then, when you least expect it, it visits again. In a memory. In a quiet moment. In something small that reminds you of what used to be. And suddenly, you feel it all over again because losing someone like that doesn't just change your life once. It changes it forever.

The cycle of grief didn't stop. If anything, it became part of how I saw myself. At some point, I started to believe something I didn't fully understand, that maybe I was meant to be the one to restore what had been lost. That my name, my existence, had to mean something more. I told myself, I will bring the smiles back. I will be the light in their world. And the only way I knew how to move forward was to work. We were always told, "Education is the key to success." So, I held onto that like it was the only thing that wouldn't leave me. I didn't fully know my talents. I didn't know where I truly belonged, but I knew one thing: I was going to succeed academically. I was going to break records. I was going to become someone the next generation could look up to and follow. It wasn't easy. Opportunities came and were taken away. Friends I trusted showed envy. Some relationships failed because I refused to lose focus. I was bullied. I was hurt by people who were supposed to guide me. Teachers. Relatives. And somehow, the hardest part wasn't always what people did. It was feeling like no one really saw me. Even with my own mother, it felt like she was there physically but far away from what I was going through. And in that distance, I started losing myself.

That spark I once had the one that believed education would open every closed door began to fade. There were moments I don't talk about easily. Moments when giving up felt like the only way to escape the pressure. Thoughts that came quietly but stayed longer than they should have. More times than I can count, I wondered if I was strong enough to keep going but somehow, I was still there. Still breathing. Still trying. Still refusing to give up, because deep down, something in me wouldn't let me stop. I knew I had to find myself again. Strangers became part of my story. People who didn't know me deeply but showed up when I needed them most. They held my hand in moments when I felt like I was falling apart. And in their presence, I found pieces of strength I didn't know I still had. And I kept telling myself, If not for me, then for them. For the ones who never got the opportunities I had. For the ones whose stories ended too soon. For the ones who are no longer here. Even if they are beyond the grave, I will carry them forward. Every loss, every disappointment, every silent battle I never spoke about it didn't disappear. It stayed. It built up. Quietly. Patiently. Layer by layer.

Chapter 3

The War Within Me

The hardest battles are not the ones people see. They are the ones that happen quietly inside you while the world calls you strong. People saw resilience. They saw determination. They saw the version of me that kept showing up, kept smiling, and kept carrying responsibilities as though nothing was breaking underneath but what they never saw was the exhaustion behind it. The nights I fought thoughts I couldn't explain. The pressure of constantly being "the strong one" when I was barely holding myself together. Somewhere along the way, strength stopped feeling empowering and started feeling like a prison. Because once people believe you are strong, they stop asking if you are okay. They assume you can survive anything. And maybe that was the war within me: trying to save everyone else while silently losing myself. I prayed for things to get better. I prayed against the depression and anxiety that slowly tried to make a home inside me. I prayed for my health because I had already seen how quickly life could change, how one moment could turn everything upside down.

I kept forgiving people who hurt me, even when the pain they caused stayed with me longer than their apologies ever did. I chose silence over retaliation, not because I was weak, but because I was tired of carrying more hate than I already had.

Still, there were nights when I asked myself, where did I go wrong? All I had ever tried to do was survive without becoming bitter. All I had ever wanted was peace. Even in the confusion, even in the moments when faith felt heavy, one thing kept me going the belief that somehow, one day, God would answer me. And maybe that hope, fragile as it was, became the reason I didn't give up. There was a war inside me that no one could see. I kept asking myself, will I ever make it in life? I had already experienced loss in so many forms. Losing family. Losing friendships. Losing relationships. At some point, it even felt like I was losing myself but there was one thing I could never imagine losing, and that was the dream of making something out of my life. Was I asking for too much? Even in my lowest moments, there was still a voice inside me that refused to die. A voice that kept whispering, If not for me, then for who? For the ones whose stories ended too soon. For the ones who never got the chance to become who they were meant to be. For the ones who left this world carrying dreams they could no longer fulfil.

Sometimes I wonder... what if God showed us our destination from the beginning? Maybe life would have been different. Maybe we wouldn't waste so much time on things that slowly destroy us. Maybe we wouldn't lose hope so easily. Maybe grief wouldn't feel so endless but life does not reveal itself all at once. Instead, it leaves you alone with your thoughts, your pain, and your unanswered questions. And the truth is, no one is ever fully prepared for that kind of battle. At some point, the goal stopped being happiness. The goal became survival. The war within me grew stronger every time an opportunity slipped through my hands. Doors would open just enough for me to see the possibility of a different life, and then suddenly, they would close again. And every time it happened, I knew it was another battle between faith and disappointment. Part of me wanted to call it a curse. Maybe it was never truly meant for me in the first place. Maybe I was chasing things God had never written my name on.

Nevertheless, another part of me, the part that refused to completely lose faith, believed that maybe God was preparing me for something greater than what I was crying over. Something deeper. Something worth more than I could understand at the time. And maybe that is the hardest part of faith, not knowing. Not knowing whether the delay is protection or rejection. Not knowing whether the pain has purpose or whether you are simply learning how to survive it. The waiting became its own kind of suffering. Wondering every day, will it ever get better? Whereas I held onto the words that raised me: the belief that God is always closest to people in pain. That somehow, suffering was not proof of His absence, but evidence of His presence. And on the days when I had no strength left to believe in myself, that belief carried me instead.

Chapter 4

The Version of Me I Had to Become

Pain has a way of changing people quietly. Not all at once, but slowly, in the small moments you barely notice until one day, you look at yourself and realise you are no longer the person you used to be. Somewhere between grief, survival, disappointment, and silence, I became a version of myself I never planned to be. Stronger, maybe. Wiser, perhaps. Yet also more guarded. More tired. More aware of how quickly life can change. The truth is that healing did not only ask me to let go of pain. It asked me to let go of the person I was before it. The version of me I had to become was not born from comfort. It was created through disappointment, silence, survival, and the slow realisation that not everyone who stands beside you is truly for you. I started isolating myself. Not because I hated people, but because life kept teaching me how dangerous it could be to depend too heavily on anyone.

Death had already shown me that people can leave without warning. Life taught me something even harder: sometimes, people are still alive yet emotionally gone. So, I began learning how to live without people while they were still here. I began learning independence, not as a choice, but as protection, because I never wanted my strength, peace, or future to depend on something life could suddenly take away from me. And slowly, my mindset changed. I stopped living only for the present version of myself. I started thinking about the generations after me. The environment I would create. The peace I never experienced but wanted others to have. Somewhere along the way, I also realised something painful, many people only know how to take. They take your kindness, your energy, your loyalty, your silence, and still ask for more without ever pouring anything back into you. Some people support you publicly but secretly compete with you in private. Others listen to your pain without ever truly trying to understand it. And eventually, you grow tired of explaining yourself to people who only hear you through their own perspectives.

So, you carry it alone. You go to bed with a heavy heart and thoughts too deep to speak out loud. You suppress the pain. You hide it inside dreams of a better tomorrow because survival becomes the only thing your mind knows how to do. I used to think life was supposed to become easier after pain. But maybe life was never designed that way. Maybe strength is built through learning how to continue even when nothing around you feels light. And still, despite everything, I kept going. There is something powerful about people witnessing your journey, watching you come from almost nothing and still choosing to build something meaningful. Not only for yourself, but for those who are gone, those who are still here, and those who are yet to come. Maybe that became my purpose, to become proof that pain does not always destroy people. Sometimes, it creates the very person who changes everything. For a long time, I thought my story was only about pain. About grief. About survival. About carrying wounds, I never asked for.

Except now I understand something differently. I was not only surviving life, but I was also becoming someone through it. Every loss, every disappointment, every silent battle shaped the version of me I had to become. Not perfect. Not unbreakable. Yet still standing. Still believing. Still choosing to continue despite everything that tried to stop me. And maybe that is what breaking generational cycles truly means. Not living without pain but refusing to let pain be the final inheritance you leave behind. So, if my story becomes proof of anything, let it be this, pain may shape a person, but it does not have to define their ending.

Chapter 5

When Faith Becomes a Choice

There are some lessons life refuses to teach you gently. Trusting God without understanding His timing was one of them. Throughout this journey, I had to learn that faith is not always about knowing what God is doing. Sometimes, faith is choosing God when you have no idea what He is doing at all. Choosing God was a decision I had to make before I understood. I searched for answers everywhere. I tried traditional healers. I looked for solutions in places I thought might explain what was happening to me, hoping someone would tell me why my life felt like a constant battle and how I could finally break free from everything that seemed to follow my family. However, nothing worked. The more I searched, the more confused I became. Then someone's son,a man rooted in faith came into my life and showed me something I had been struggling to understand only God knows. I will forever be grateful that I met him. He did not have to fight my battles for me. He simply helped me see that perhaps I had been looking for answers everywhere except where I needed to look.

I had spent so much time trying to understand the battle that I forgot to ask who I was trusting to carry me through it. That moment became a turning point. I chose God not because everything suddenly became easier. Not because every prayer was immediately answered. Not because the depression disappeared, my academic struggles ended, friendships stopped hurting, or the disappointments suddenly made sense. I chose God because I realised I could not build my life around constantly trying to understand Him. The goal had always been bigger than university. Being in varsity was only the beginning. I had convinced myself that getting there would be the moment everything changed. I thought education would be the key that unlocked every door. I thought that once I reached that point, the rest of the story would somehow fall into place.

University became a place where I discovered that achieving a dream does not mean you stop fighting. I still had to deal with depression. I still had academic pressure. I still experienced betrayal from people I trusted. I watched friendships change. I encountered envy from people I never expected it from. I experienced disappointments that made me question whether the people around me were really happy to see me succeed.

And then there was love.

Somewhere along the way, I had believed that love would find me, too. I had imagined that there would be someone who would choose me, understand me, and make the journey feel a little less lonely. Whereas that did not happen the way I imagined. I had dreams I prayed about, plans I believed in, and a picture of the life I thought I would have by a certain point. Yet life kept reminding me that my timeline and God's timing were not the same thing. That was difficult to accept. I had to learn that a delay is not always a denial. Sometimes, the dream is still alive, it is simply taking longer than you expected. So, I sat down and drew a new vision board. It was no longer just a board filled with things I wanted. It became a declaration of the person I wanted to become. A reminder that my life was still moving, even when it did not look like it. Maybe the delay was necessary. Maybe there were things I needed to heal before I could hold what I had been praying for. Maybe there were parts of me that needed to grow before I could step into the future, I kept asking God for.

I began to understand that purpose is not always about changing everyone else. For so long, I thought breaking generational curses meant becoming successful enough to rescue everyone behind me and everyone coming after me. I thought I had to carry the whole family, restore every broken thing, and become the answer to every unanswered question. However, God began teaching me something different. Sometimes, purpose begins with becoming whole yourself. How can I build a healthier future if I am still bleeding from the past? How can I teach the next generation peace if I have never learned how to sit with my own pain? How can I tell people that they can become more than what happened to them if I am still allowing what happened to me to define who I am? I had to stop seeing myself only as the person who survived. I had to start becoming the person who healed. And healing was not clean. Faith did not erase my grief. It did not mean I stopped being angry. It did not mean I stopped doubting. It did not mean I stopped becoming exhausted.

I learned that faith could exist alongside all of those things. I could love God and still have questions. I could believe and still cry. I could pray and still feel tired. I could trust Him and still not understand why the answer was taking so long. For years, I thought doubt meant my faith was weak. Now I understand that sometimes doubt is simply what happens when a human being is trying to make sense of something bigger than they can understand. God was not asking me to understand everything. He was asking me to trust Him. And that became one of the hardest choices I have ever made. I began looking at my scars differently, too. For a long time, I saw them as evidence that something was wrong with me. I looked at everything I had survived and wondered why I had to experience so much.

Now I see them differently.

My scars are evidence that I survived. They are reminders of the nights I thought I would not make it and somehow woke up the next morning. They are reminders of losses that nearly broke me, disappointments that changed me, betrayals that taught me discernment, and prayers I thought would never be answered. They are not proof that I am broken. They are proof that I kept going. And perhaps that is what God was teaching me all along. I thought He was delaying my life. I thought He was withholding the things I had prayed for. I thought the silence meant rejection. Still maybe the delay was also preparation. Maybe the unanswered questions were teaching me to trust without control. Maybe the closed doors were teaching me that my destiny could not depend on everything going according to my plan. And maybe choosing God was never supposed to be a one-time decision.

Maybe I would have to choose Him again when the dream was delayed. Again, when depression became heavy, friendships betrayed me, love did not come when I expected it to, academics became overwhelming, grief returned, doubt became louder than faith, and I had no explanation. I had to choose Him in the middle of the waiting because faith is easy when everything makes sense. The real test was learning to say, "I still trust You," when nothing made sense at all. I don't have every answer now, but I no longer need every answer to keep walking. The dream may be delayed, but I believe it is not denied. The scars may remain, but they no longer tell me that I am broken. The grief may visit, but it no longer gets to decide who I become. And the purpose I once thought was about changing everyone around me has become something much deeper. It begins with me becoming healed, becoming the person who can finally say, I survived what was meant to destroy me. And somehow, through it all, I still chose God.

Chapter 6

The Dream That Refused to Die

The dream within me still refuses to die. It did not disappear. It simply changed shape. For a long time, I thought my dream was only about achieving certain things, ticking certain boxes, and proving that I could become someone despite where I came from. However somewhere along the journey, I realised that the dream was much deeper than that. It was never only about success. It was about becoming the person God created me to be. I refuse to let depression, betrayal, rejection, and delays become my identity. They are chapters of my story, but they are not the whole story. They are things I experienced. They are not who I am. For a long time, my motivation came from wanting to prove people wrong. I wanted to show the people who doubted me that they were wrong about me. I wanted my achievements to speak for every time I had been underestimated, misunderstood, rejected, or overlooked.

Yet eventually, I realised something. Living to prove everyone wrong still gives them power over you. If every achievement is about proving someone wrong, then your life is still being shaped by the people who doubted you. I no longer want that. I don't want to become successful just so someone can look at me and regret ever underestimating me. I want to become who God created me to be. That shift changed everything. It meant that my journey was no longer about revenge. It was about purpose. And perhaps one of the hardest things I had to accept was that I might have to make it alone. Not because I had no one. I was blessed with a support system, and I will never take that for granted. There were people who showed up for me, people who believed in me, and people who helped me carry things when I couldn't carry them alone. Still there were also parts of my journey I had to walk by myself. I learned that when you tell people about your problems, they sometimes understand them according to their own perceptions. They hear your story through the lens of their experiences and assumptions.

Sometimes, they over-identify with what you are saying. Sometimes, they minimise it. Sometimes, they make you feel as though you are making excuses. And sometimes, you can explain yourself over and over again and still walk away feeling misunderstood. That happened to me more times than I can count. I wasn't always looking for someone to fix me. Sometimes, I just wanted someone to understand what I was trying to say. I wanted someone to hear the weight behind the words but I learned that not everyone has the capacity to understand a pain they have never carried. And that was another lesson in independence. I had to stop waiting for everyone to understand me before I allowed myself to move forward. I had to learn how to validate my own experience. How to acknowledge that what hurt me was real, even if someone else didn't see it. How to keep going even when my story was misunderstood.

Maybe that is what growing up really is, learning that not everyone will understand your journey and choosing to walk it anyway. God has blessed me with twenty-one years of grace. I don't want to romanticise my life and pretend that it has been easy. It hasn't. There have been moments when I have questioned everything. There have been losses I didn't understand, disappointments I didn't expect, and seasons when simply continuing required more strength than I thought I had but when I look back, I also see grace. I see the things I survived. I see the doors that opened. I see the people God placed in my life. I see the support I received when I needed it. And I see the boxes I have already been able to tick from the dream I once only imagined. That matters.

Sometimes, we become so focused on what has not happened yet that we forget to thank God for what has already happened. I am learning to celebrate the small victories. The things that once lived only on my vision board are slowly becoming part of my reality. Not everything. Not yet. Although some. And maybe that is enough to remind me that the dream is still alive. I am still young. Just a young girl from the dusty roads of a village, carrying bigger visions than the place I came from ever taught me to imagine. Sometimes, I think about where I started and where I want to go, and the distance between the two can feel intimidating. However, I refuse to believe that where I come from determines how far I can go. Those dusty roads are part of my story. They are not the boundary of my future. I don't want to run away from where I came from as though it is something I should be ashamed of. I want to carry it with me. I want the little girl from those roads to look at the woman I become one day and know that her dreams were not foolish.

I want the young ones coming after me to see someone from where they come from doing things they were told were impossible. I want them to know that bigger dreams are allowed. That the village, poverty, rejection, depression, betrayal, and delays do not have to be the end of the story. I want them to look at me and say, "If she could do it, maybe I can, too. "And that is when I understood that my dream was never only mine. There is something bigger attached to it. Every time I keep going, I am showing someone else that it is possible. Every box I tick becomes evidence. Every door I walk through becomes an invitation for someone else to believe that they can walk through theirs, too. Maybe this is part of breaking the cycle. Not only breaking what hurt us but creating evidence that a different life is possible. I don't know exactly where this dream will take me. I don't know how long it will take. I don't know how many more delays, disappointments, or unexpected turns are waiting along the way but I know this the dream within me is still alive.

And as long as God continues to give me breath, I refuse to bury it. I am no longer running to prove everyone wrong. I am running towards the person God created me to become. And if the young girl from the dusty roads of that village can become a testimony of what is possible, then perhaps someone else will dare to dream, too. Maybe that is the legacy I want to leave behind. Not that I had an easy life, but that I had a difficult one and still dared to dream. Still dared to believe, to become, and to shine. Just because it has not happened yet does not mean it will never happen. Just because the door has not opened does not mean you were wrong to knock. And just because your life looks different from what you imagined by now does not mean you have failed.

Chapter 7

You Are Not Too Late to Begin Again

Sometimes, life takes us through seasons we never planned for. Seasons of waiting, uncertainty, disappointment, and quiet rebuilding. Seasons when we watch other people become everything they once hoped to be while we are still trying to understand why our own journey seems to be taking so long. And if we are not careful, we begin to mistake the delay for a denial. We begin to question the dreams that once made our hearts feel alive. We wonder whether we should let them go, whether we have missed our opportunity, or whether perhaps we were never meant for the things we once believed were possible whereas not everything that takes time is lost. Some dreams require preparation. Some require courage we have not yet developed, wisdom we have not yet acquired, or a version of ourselves we are still learning to become. Others may need to change shape as we grow, because sometimes the life we imagine at the beginning is not the life that will ultimately bring us peace.

And sometimes, we simply do not know why things take as long as they do. That uncertainty can be difficult to carry. It is hard to keep believing when you have prayed the same prayer for years. It is hard to remain hopeful when you have tried, failed, started again, and watched another season pass without the breakthrough you were hoping for. It is hard to explain to people why you still believe in something they think you should have outgrown by now but you do not owe anyone an apology for still having hope. You are allowed to want the life you once imagined. You are allowed to pursue the degree, build the business, write the book, find the love, create the home, or become the person you have always carried in your heart. You are allowed to begin again, even when beginning again feels embarrassing. You are allowed to change your plans without abandoning your purpose. And you are allowed to grieve the time you have lost without believing that all your opportunities are gone. Perhaps you are not where you thought you would be by now. Perhaps there are things you hoped to have accomplished by this age, this year, or this season. Perhaps you have had to watch some of your plans fall apart and gather the courage to imagine something new.

Give yourself grace.

You have lived through things your younger self could not have anticipated. You have carried responsibilities, navigated disappointments, learned difficult lessons, and survived days that demanded more from you than anyone could see. You are not standing still simply because your progress does not look the way you expected. Sometimes, you are building the strength to sustain what you once only had the courage to desire. Remember this, too, you do not have to wait passively for your dreams to find you. Hope can be gentle, but it can also be active. You can trust the process while taking the next step. You can pray while preparing. You can believe in your future while making practical decisions that bring you closer to it. You can honour a dream without demanding that it happen on your schedule. And if the dream changes along the way, that does not make the person who first dreamed it foolish. It simply means you are growing, and growth sometimes teaches us to want different things.

The goal is not to hold on to every dream at any cost. The goal is to never let disappointment convince you that your life has no more possibilities. So, if you are reading this while waiting for something you once thought would have happened by now, let this be your reminder, you are not too late to begin again. You are not too old to learn something new. You are not a failure because your journey has taken longer than you expected. And you do not have to be ashamed of the distance between where you are and where you hoped to be. Keep making room for possibility. Keep showing up for the life you want to build. Keep allowing yourself to dream, even if you have to dream differently than before. There is still life to live while you wait. There is still joy to experience, love to receive, wisdom to gather, and beauty to discover in the person you are becoming. Do not postpone your happiness until every prayer is answered. Do not withhold kindness from yourself until every goal is achieved. Do not treat the present as a waiting room for the future.

Your dreams matter, but so does the person carrying them.

And as you continue to become, may you learn to hold your dreams with hope rather than desperation, with patience rather than shame, and with enough faith to keep moving even when you cannot yet see how everything will unfold. Your story is still being written. Your possibilities are not exhausted. And the life you are building can still surprise you. Some things take longer than we hoped. Some doors open differently than we imagined. Some dreams ask us to grow before we can fully understand them but until life tells us otherwise, we can keep showing up, keep doing the work, and keep making space for what is possible.

Chapter 8

The Fragments That Still Shine

I stopped chasing what glitters.i kept what shines— Shards

After everything, it's not about what was lost, but what stayed. A twenty-one-year-old girl from the dusty roads of a village, pursuing a degree at one of the best universities in South Africa, the great University of Johannesburg. Barely coping, driven by the urge to help people understand and cope with their own psyches while still navigating her own loss and grief. A girl whose "no" was not heard. A girl who lived with people who envied her and competed with her. A girl who stopped bothering anyone with her feelings because they were misinterpreted and misunderstood. Holding on to the little support she gets. Embracing her solo era. Constantly worrying, What if I don't make it? Wanting to have her own things and live an unfiltered life. Wanting to turn crumbs into a crown. Yearning for an all-encompassing love that heals her childhood traumas. Wanting to become the best version of herself without pretending, and the best she can be for her family because she believes they deserve far better than what life has offered them.

Sometimes, I wonder what it would be like if a person could watch their life like a film, rewind it, change certain scenes, and make the best out of it.

I was born for a purpose. I believe my name is my destination, and I should live accordingly. At the end of the day, I have come to understand that life is a journey. I am forever grateful for the people who make that journey better without me even having to beg them to be there. There is an urge within me to become a better version of the person my community, family, parents, and friends may have struggled to become. People often want others to live according to their expectations and experiences, constantly neglecting their emotions and controlling their choices without realising the consequences. No one talks about how hard it is to develop a sense of identity. The kindest thing you can do for yourself is to define who you are as an individual. All in all, no matter how broken you are, you still have the ability to shine within you.

Give yourself the little grace you deserve. You are trying your best, and that matters. It doesn't have to make sense to anyone else.
